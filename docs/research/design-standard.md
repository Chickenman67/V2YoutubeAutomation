# Design Standard: The Paint Explainer in Finance

**Status:** Research deliverable (no code). Source: design research, issue #2.
**Scope:** Animated explainer videos for a finance audience that look like a designer made them — not a screen-recorder or an AI slideshow.

## 1. Watchlist studied (real, observed 2026-08)

| Channel | Subs | Videos | Typical length | Subject & style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Paint Explainer (`@ThePaintExplainer`) | 1.96M | 161 | 11–16 min | History/mystery/space listicles: *"Ancient Greek Myths That Turned Out to Be True"*, *"The Most Disturbing Creatures in Each Ocean Layer"*. Painted-scene montage with punchy headline captions. |
| Economics Explained (`@EconomicsExplained`) | 2.87M | 442 | 13–47 min | Country/policy finance: *"Where Has Venezuela's Oil Gone?"*, *"Why Is the World In So Much Debt"*. Talking head + stock footage + bold stat overlays + maps. |
| Two Cents / PBS (`@TwoCentsPBS`) | 824K | 266 | 6–11 min | Personal finance animated: *"Tariffs, Explained By PBS"*, *"Why Gift Cards Are Good For Stores, Bad For You"*. Pure 2D flat-vector cartoon, two recurring host characters, kinetic type. |

**Observed pattern that wins:** the channels with the largest per-video audience on abstract subjects (history, finance) all broadcast a *curiosity-gap hook* ("X That Turned Out to Be Y"), keep episodes under ~16 min the moment visuals are pure illustration, and repeat a single recognisable visual identity across every thumbnail + frame.

The **benchmark for "looks designed"** is a hybrid of The Paint Explainer's framing/pacing and Two Cents' flat-vector craft. Economics Explained is the lesson in what *not* to copy for a designed look (it leans on real footage and a live host).

## 2. Recommendation: Adopt-as-standard (blend The Paint Explainer + Two Cents)

- **Adopt wholesale from Two Cents:** flat 2D vector illustration, recurring cast/silhouettes, limited bright palette, kinetic typography, chapter cards, no visible narrator.
- **Adopt from The Paint Explainer:** curiosity-gap episode framing, punched headline captions per beat, decisive scene-by-scene cuts, english-forward explanatory montage.
- **Adapt for finance:** replace crime/history shock content with markets, macro, and personal-finance stakes; swap the "disturbing" tone for **intrigue + consequence**; keep every on-screen number exact and sourced (finance punishes visual shorthand that implies a wrong figure).

## 3. Visual language

### 3.1 Illustration style — flat vector, not paint, not 3D
- **Do:** flat 2D vector shapes with strong silhouettes, soft shading (1–2 tints, no gradients-overkill), rounded geometry. Two Cents is the reference: characters readable in profile, single consistent stroke weight ~2px, generous "safe" negative space.
- **Do:** a **recurring cast** (2–3 recurring characters, e.g. a protagonist "investor" + antagonist "uncertainty") so the audience builds recognition — the single strongest "designed" signal a channel can have.
- **Don't:** photorealism, heavy 3D renders, or AI video. Stock footage (Economics Explained's crutch) reads as *assembled*, not *designed*. Use it only for OCR-neutral establishing shots.

### 3.2 Thumbnail discipline (the front door)
- One hero subject + one short curiosity phrase, max ~6 words, in the house font (below).
- Subjects are usually a "painted/illustrated" object rather than a chart. Economics Explained thumbnails that chart-first still retain a stylised illustrative hero.
- Lock the palette (3.4) and cast so every thumbnail is recognisably "ours".

## 4. Pacing

- **Target runtime: 7–12 min.** Two Cents holds 6–11 (pure-illustration fatigue ceiling); The Paint Explainer's 11–16 works only because their beats are shocking. Finance rewards tightness: aim ~1 min per concept, ~10 min cap.
- **Cut on the beat, not on the breath.** The Paint Explainer holds one "painting" ~3–6s then cuts hard — momentum generated by decisive cuts, not speed. Use scene cuts per clause, and a **one-beat pause** after any money figure so it lands.
- **Section cadence:** hook (≤30s) → thesis → 4–7 numbered beats → payoff/recap card. Reuse an **end-of-video recap card** (Two Cents pattern) with the single takeaway.
- **No talking head fills time.** If a host face appears, it is a 2–4s illustrative cameo, never the primary medium.

## 5. Typography

- **Body:** one geometric sans, medium weight, e.g. Montserrat / Poppins (Two Cents-style rounded geometric). Avoid serif body in an explainer; reserve serif for a single editorial "thesis" line.
- **Emphasis:** bold + a warm accent colour (from palette) on the **keyword** of each sentence. This is the Two Cents signature: partial-bolding ("Why **GIFT CARDS** are good for stores, bad for **YOU**"). Do this on virtually every caption.
- **Numbers/money:** always tabular figures, accent-coloured, sized bigger than surrounding words. Right-align monetary values to a baseline. Never let a figure auto-wrap.
- **Sizes (at 1080p):** headline 72–96px, caption 44–56px, footnote/source 24px. Minimum legible on mobile thumbnails: keep headline readable at ~180px wide.

## 6. Colour palette

Adopt a **limited, bright, high-contrast** flat design palette (Two Cents teaches restraint — ~5 colours max per video, same roles every time):

- **Background:** warm off-white / very light paper (`#F7F4EF`) as the default canvas; inverse dark (`#12151C`) reserved for "risk/crisis" beats.
- **Ink / text:** near-black charcoal (`#1B1F27`).
- **Primary action colour** (money/positive): a warm green or teal (`#1F9D82`-ish) — only for positive capital flows and growth.
- **Accent / warnings** (debt/risk/negative): a warm red/coral (`#E4572E`-ish) — only for loss, defaults, risk.
- **Gold highlight** for "wow" figures/opportunity.
- **Semantic lock:** one money colour, one risk colour, one highlight — reused identically across *all* videos. Two Cents' whole "designed" feel is that colour carries meaning.

Do **not** use the market-automation red/green tape convention *and* the narrative accent colours interchangeably — pick one convention and stick to it (recommend narrative: green = growth, red = risk, in addition to a contrasting action colour).

## 7. White space

- Finance content is dense; **white/empty space is the anti-noise weapon.** One idea per frame. Only draw the shapes necessary to that clause; fade everything else to background tone.
- Constrain graphics to a central "stage" (safe zone ~775×380 of 16:9), keep the outer ~10% margin empty for caption breathing room and subtitle clearance.
- Between beats use a clean full-frame transition (blank background, single word) rather than cluttered dissolve — this is the single cheapest way to look art-directed.

## 8. On-screen caption treatment

- **One sentence, one meaning, partial-bolding** (see 5). Never a paragraph.
- Captions either **track the voiceover verbatim** (hard-subs for accessibility, small) **or** surface only the *keywords* (punchy, large). Do Keyword-captions by default; keep full verbatim subtitles via YouTube CC.
- A figure that is spoken **must** appear on screen in the same second — no "he said X but screen shows Y". Finance viewers check.
- Always show **source/footnote** (24px) for any statistic: "Source: BLS, 2025". This is a credibility multiplier finance audiences judge on.

## 9. What we deliberately reject
- Screencasters / terminal footage as primary content (reads as "not designed").
- Clause-after-clause of stock video (Economics Explained's authorentic-but-un-designed look).
- Long runtime bloat (15+ min) unless demanded by true breadth; prefer tighter episodes done more often.
- Any on-screen number that cannot be traced to a source.

## 10. One-paragraph summary
Adopt a Two Cents flat-vector craft (recurring cast, ~5-colour semantic palette over a paper background, geometric sans with partial-bolding, generous white space, chapter/recap cards) fused with The Paint Explainer's decisive cut-on-the-beat pacing and curiosity-gap framing, adapted to finance by keeping episodes 7–12 min, replacing shock with consequence/stakes, and enforcing a hard rule that every spoken figure appears on-screen with a visible source — producing an explainer that reads as deliberately designed and finance-credible rather than assembled.
